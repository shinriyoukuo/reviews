SCRIPTYour opening script & flow
  1. Hand wash! (ABHR, 20–30 seconds)
  2. Introduce yourself.
    "Hello, good afternoon! My name is [your name] and I will be your nurse today."
  3. Ask the patient's name and DOB.
    "May I please know your name? … And your date of birth please?"
  4. Ask if they know their URN/Medicare number, and ask about allergies.
    "Okay Mr. Ivan, do you know your URN or Medicare number? … And do you have any allergies?"
  5. Safety check the room. Oxygen, suction and call bell working? Bed brakes on? Railings not stuck? No trip hazards?
  6. Explain the procedure and gain consent.
    "Okay Mr. Ivan, I'll do some necessary assessments to give you the appropriate care that you'll need. I'll explain the process as I go and some may require me to touch you. Is that okay with you? … Just tell me if you get uncomfortable or something is hurting so I can immediately stop. Is that alright, Mr. Ivan?"
  7. Perform the A to E assessment.
  8. Head-to-toe assessment.
ABCDEPrimary assessment

A: Airway

  • Observe how he is breathing and whether anything is obstructing the airway. Breathing is normally silent. Audible breathing may mean an obstruction.
  • Checklist 4.7.1: no abnormal sound while the patient is speaking = clear airway.
  • Your note: if something is blocking it, remove the foreign object (your notes say forceps). If the airway is clear, keep it open with the head-tilt–chin-lift method. Call the RN.
  • Why? So we know whether the patient is breathing properly.
  • Document: Clear / Obstruction

B: Breathing

  • Count RR for a full minute.
  • Check the trachea is midline by running your finger down the neck.
  • Look for accessory muscle use, grunting and abnormal breathing patterns. If present: try repositioning the patient.
  • Normal (4.7.2): regular, equal rise and fall of the chest, no shortness of breath, no oxygen therapy, no cyanosis.
  • Why? To see if the patient is having difficulty breathing.
  • Document: Normal / Abnormal. If abnormal: SOB, increased WOB, irregular, apnoea.

C: Circulation

  • Vital signs: temperature, BP (manual), HR, RR, SpO₂. The assessor gives you the values to document.
  • Pulse: regularity, equality, strength. Warmth of all 4 limbs. Capillary refill of fingers and toes. Swelling of the lower extremities.
  • Your notes also put pain and urinalysis here (pain: ask, document, adjust the care).

D: Disability

  • Your note: this is where you ask if you can do the Neurological Obs Chart.
  • AVPU, GCS, PEARL (pupils), AMT4, BGL. Your notes add limb strength, medications and history.

E: Exposure

  • Ask about and check for pain, skin tears, rash, swelling, bruising, an IV cannula, or any pre-existing injury. If the patient seems confused, check physically.
  • Ivan has a chronic wound on his right leg. Note it.
  • Why? To find out if any treatment is needed.
  • Document: Pain / Pressure sore / Pressure injury / Skin tears / Swelling / Ulcer / Nil exposure

NEURODisability tools: AVPU, GCS, PEARL, AMT4, limbs

AVPU checks whether they are conscious, or reacting only to voice or pain, or unresponsive. A = Alert · V = responds to Voice · P = responds to Pain only · U = Unconscious/unresponsive.

GCSOptions on the form
Eye (E1–4)Spontaneously 4 · To speech 3 · To pain 2 · None 1
Verbal (V1–5)Oriented 5 · Confused 4 · Inappropriate words 3 · Incomprehensible sounds 2 · None 1
Motor (M1–6)Obeys commands 6 · Localises to pain 5 · Withdraws 4 · Flexion to pain 3 · Extension to pain 2 · None 1

Total out of 15, calculated by hand. Normal is 15/15 and the lowest is 3.

PEARL: Pupils Equal And Reactive to Light (PEARLA adds Accommodation). Shine a pen torch into each eye and check the reaction. Document the size of each eye (1–8 mm; normal 3–5) and PEARL yes/no for each eye.

AMT4: (1) full name & DOB, which you already asked, (2) age, (3) month & year, (4) name of the place. If he gets any of the 4 wrong, the RN or physician does a further delirium assessment. That isn't part of this task.

Limb strength (each arm and leg): ask them to pull, push, close and open their hands/arms. Rate on the neuro chart: 5 normal power · 4 active movement against resistance · 3 against gravity · 2 with gravity eliminated · 1 flicker · 0 none. Speech: hoarseness, slurred (dysarthria), stuttering, normal.

Neurovascular obs (all 4 limbs): extremity, oedema, colour, temperature, capillary refill, sensation, motion, pain score, pulse (radial for the arms, dorsalis pedis for the legs).

BGL: checks for hypo/hyperglycaemia (normal 4–8). Your note: don't use an alcohol swab. Ask the patient to wash their hands.

Ask about medications: "What type of medicine do you take, Mr. Jones?" and history: "May I ask for your medical history? And have you had any surgery before?"

TESTSUrinalysis, BGL, BMI, histories, vaccines

Urinalysis (12 steps, 4.16)

  1. Gather equipment: urinalysis kit, non-sterile gloves, paper towel, sample urine container, 3 ml syringe
  2. Hand hygiene with ABHR, put on gloves
  3. Inspect the colour and odour of the sample
  4. Check the expiry date of the test strips
  5. Draw up the sample with a sterile syringe
  6. Place the strip on a paper towel and apply urine from the syringe, covering every pad
  7. Remove excess urine from the strip
  8. Wait 2 minutes before reading
  9. Hold the strip horizontally and compare with the colour chart on the bottle
  10. Record the results
  11. If there are signs of UTI, don't dispose of the urine. Keep it sterile by closing the lid.
  12. Remove gloves, hand hygiene with ABHR

Form fields: specific gravity (1.000–1.030), pH (5–8.5), leucocytes, blood, nitrite, ketones, bilirubin, urobilinogen, protein, glucose. Positive nitrite + leucocytes = possible UTI, which the assessor will prompt.

Other measurements

  • BGL: assessor prompts 10.2 mmol/L (above 4–8 = hyperglycaemia, report to RN). The form also asks for blood ketones.
  • Height + weight → BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
  • Vaccinations: influenza (flu), shingles, pneumococcal polysaccharide (23vPPV)

Histories (4.8–4.12)

  • Allergies: medication, food, other (pollen, bee sting…)
  • Medical history, by system: CNS, CVS, respiratory, GI, renal, MSK, integumentary, endocrine, sensory
  • Surgical history: CVS, respiratory, GI, renal
  • Medication history (use the resident's medication list)
  • Family/relationship: marital status, next of kin, NOK relationship, lifestyle (alcohol, exercise, smoker)
  • Reason for admission (e.g. fall)

Equipment for assessments (4.5): automatic BP machine, stethoscope, pen torch, BGL kit, urinalysis kit, weighing machine, height measuring tape.

RISKBraden, FRAT, MST, 4AT, skin

Pressure injury risk: Braden Scale. Sensory perception, moisture, activity, mobility, nutrition, friction & shear. A lower score means higher risk.

Falls risk: FRAT. Reasons for assessment: admission, change in condition, change of location, post fall, doctor's order. High risk if ANY: a fall in the past 12 months · unsteady or uses a walking aid · confused or answers any AMT4 question wrong · urinary/faecal frequency, urgency or nocturia · agitation/aggression. Moderate = none of these but may still be at risk. Ivan = HIGH.

Malnutrition: MST. Lost weight recently without trying? No 0 / Unsure 2 / Yes, how much: 1–3 kg 1 · 4–6 kg 2 · 7–10 kg 3 · >10 kg 4. Eating poorly because of decreased appetite? No 0 / Yes 1. Total ≥ 2 = at risk.

Delirium: 4AT. Alertness (normal 0, clearly abnormal 4) · AMT4 (0 mistakes 0 / 1 mistake 1 / 2+ 2) · Attention: months backwards from December (7+ correct 0 / starts but <7 1 / untestable 2) · Acute change or fluctuating course (no 0 / yes 4). ≥4 possible delirium · 1–3 possible cognitive impairment · 0 unlikely.

Comprehensive skin assessment: temperature, moisture (dry), turgor (normal <3 s), integrity (fragile, wound…), wound locations, skin colour taking ethnicity into account, altered sensation, medical devices. Actions: skin protection strategies, pressure redistribution surface, wound assessment, education. Referrals: allied health, stomal therapy nurse, wound care nurse/CNS, doctor.

  • Regular GP review of his wound, BGL and BP; access to physio, podiatrist and diabetes educator.
  • Meals, help with hygiene, and social activities. Family can visit.

FIXSmall fix-ups to your handwritten notes

  • RR isn't in your notes: 12–20 per minute, counted for a full minute.
  • Disability: the OSCA document also wants AMT4 (name & DOB, age, month & year, place).
